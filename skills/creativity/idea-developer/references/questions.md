# Question bank

The interview is adaptive. Each theme below gives you the goal, a core question or two, branches for how answers typically go, and the weak spot to probe. Don't ask everything in a theme. Pick the one question that moves you forward and let the answer pick the branch.

## Problem

Goal: establish that the idea sits on a real problem, one a person would recognize on their own.

Core questions:
- Walk me through a day where this problem shows up. What does it actually look like?
- What do people do right now instead of using your thing?

Branches:
- If they describe a specific ritual ("I check three sites every morning"), the problem is real. Go to Audience.
- If they describe a scenario they invented, treat the problem as unverified and ask who else you could talk to.

Probe: if the answer never leaves the idea itself ("it would be cool if..."), the problem has no owner. Say so.

## Audience

Goal: a specific user, ideally one person they can name.

Core questions:
- Who exactly feels this? Not the abstract market, the person.
- How would that person find out your thing exists?

Branches:
- "Everyone" answers get the standard push: "Everyone" isn't a user. Pick one person, maybe a friend or a colleague, and describe their Tuesday.
- If they name a group they're part of, that's a strong signal. Confirm they'd be the first user.

Probe: a user they can't reach is a distribution problem hiding inside the audience answer.

## Solution

Goal: the shape of the product and the core action, not the feature list.

Core questions:
- What's the first thing a user does with this? Walk through the first five minutes.
- What is the one action the whole thing exists to enable?

Branches:
- A confident walk-through means you can move to Scope.
- A feature list ("it does A, B, C...") means the core action is missing. Ask what the simplest version of one feature would look like.

Probe: if the first five minutes involve an account signup before any value, flag that as a real drop-off point, not a detail.

## Differentiators

Goal: an honest answer to "why would anyone switch."

Core questions:
- What do people use today instead, and what's wrong with it?
- If the obvious alternative improved by 10 percent, would your thing still matter?

Branches:
- "There's nothing like this" is usually wrong. Ask what the closest thing is and what it does poorly.
- "It's better" needs specifics. Better how, and for whom?

Probe: differentiators that live in features get copied. Differentiators in distribution, data, or relationships are stickier. Note which one they're offering.

## Scope and MVP

Goal: the smallest thing that proves the idea.

Core questions:
- If you had to ship in a month, what would you cut?
- What's the one thing that would convince you the idea has legs?

Branches:
- If the scope keeps growing ("it also needs..."), treat each addition as a separate future phase and park it.
- If the MVP is still three products, ask which one product, if it worked, would make the others worth building.

Probe: the MVP must include the risky assumption, not the safe parts. If the plan avoids the part that could fail, that's the part to move first.

## Risks

Goal: the assumptions that would kill the idea if wrong.

Core questions:
- What's the single assumption this whole thing depends on?
- What would you learn in the first week that would change the plan?

Branches:
- Naming a real assumption (people pay for it, people find it) is the point. Then ask how they'd test it cheaply.
- If the risks list is empty, push: an idea with no failure mode is an idea nobody has thought about hard.

Probe: risks they can't test without building are different from risks they can test in a week. Separate them.

## Next actions

Goal: the conversation converts into steps someone can take.

Core questions:
- What can you do in the next seven days that moves this forward?
- Who is one person you could show this to, to check the problem?

Branches:
- If they name a concrete step, lock it to a day and a definition of done.
- If they hesitate, the plan is missing a resource, time, skill, or permission. Name that gap before moving on.

Probe: a next action that depends on money or a team they don't have isn't a next action. It's a wish. Keep the steps to what one person can do.
